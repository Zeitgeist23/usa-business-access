Frequently Asked Questions

What is an offshore corporation?

An offshore corporation is a company incorporated under the laws of a jurisdiction outside the owner’s home country. It remains subject to the laws, taxes, disclosures, banking rules, and reporting obligations that apply to its owners and activities.

Is an offshore corporation legal?

Yes, when incorporated, disclosed, funded, operated, and reported lawfully. It cannot be used to hide ownership, evade tax, conceal funds, or avoid sanctions, licensing, anti-money-laundering, or reporting rules.

Why choose a corporation rather than an LLC?

A corporation may better fit businesses that need formal board governance, multiple share classes, outside investors, transferable shares, subsidiaries, or institutional counterparties. The correct structure depends on the jurisdictions and the intended activity.

Will an offshore corporation eliminate U.S. taxes?

No. U.S. persons and corporations may face extensive tax and information-reporting obligations based on ownership, control, income, accounts, transactions, and classification. Independent U.S. tax advice is essential before incorporation.

Can an offshore corporation open a bank account?

Potentially, but approval is never guaranteed. Banks review jurisdiction, shareholders, directors, beneficial owners, source of funds, business purpose, expected activity, counterparties, tax records, and compliance risk.

Does USABA provide legal or tax advice?

No. USABA provides business consulting, planning support, and professional coordination. Incorporation, constitutional documents, legal opinions, tax classifications, filings, and advice must come from qualified independent professionals.
