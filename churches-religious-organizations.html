Frequently Asked Questions

Must a church, synagogue, mosque, or temple incorporate as a nonprofit?

Federal tax law does not require every house of worship to incorporate, but a state nonprofit corporation can provide an organized legal structure for governance, contracts, property, banking, and continuity. State-specific advice should come from a qualified attorney.

Must a house of worship apply to the IRS for 501(c)(3) recognition?

Churches—including synagogues, temples, and mosques—that meet federal requirements may be treated as tax exempt without filing Form 1023, but many seek an IRS determination letter for banking, donors, grants, and institutional verification. Other religious organizations generally follow the applicable exemption application process.

Which religious organizations does USABA support?

We coordinate formation and compliance readiness for churches, synagogues, mosques, temples, congregations, ministries, religious schools, faith-based charities, and interfaith organizations.

Can a religious organization have voting members?

Yes, depending on state law and its governing documents. The bylaws should clearly define membership eligibility, voting rights, meetings, leadership selection, and amendment authority.

What records should a religious organization maintain?

Typical records include articles, bylaws, leadership resolutions, meeting minutes, contribution and restricted-fund records, budgets, payroll and housing records, property documents, insurance, and applicable filings.

Does USABA provide legal, tax, or religious advice?

No. USABA provides business consulting and coordination and can connect clients with independent legal, tax, accounting, payroll, insurance, and other qualified professionals.
