Frequently Asked Questions

What is an irrevocable life insurance trust?

An ILIT is generally an irrevocable trust designed to own one or more life insurance policies and administer policy proceeds under its terms. The result depends on the document, ownership history, insured, trustee, beneficiaries, governing law, and tax rules.

Can an existing policy be transferred to an ILIT?

Potentially, but ownership, valuation, gift-tax treatment, the insured’s retained rights, carrier requirements, and the federal three-year rule may matter. The transfer should be reviewed by qualified legal, tax, and insurance professionals before action.

Who pays the policy premiums?

The trustee generally pays premiums using trust funds. If another person contributes those funds, gift, notice, timing, and documentation requirements may apply.

What are Crummey notices?

They are written notices commonly used when beneficiaries receive temporary withdrawal rights over contributions. Whether they are appropriate and how they must be handled requires legal and tax guidance.

Does an ILIT guarantee estate-tax exclusion?

No. Results depend on ownership, retained incidents of ownership, transfer timing, document terms, administration, applicable law, and the insured’s facts. An ILIT should never be presented as an automatic tax result.

Does USABA sell insurance or provide legal or tax advice?

No. USABA provides business consulting, planning support, and professional coordination. Insurance recommendations, trust drafting, and specialized legal or tax advice must come from qualified independent professionals.
