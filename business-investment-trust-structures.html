Frequently Asked Questions

What is a business or investment trust?

It is a trust arrangement used to hold, manage, operate, or invest assets under a trust instrument and applicable state law. The legal form, powers, ownership interests, tax treatment, and regulatory status depend on the structure and facts.

How is it different from an LLC or corporation?

Trustee authority, beneficial interests, fiduciary duties, transfer rules, investor rights, tax classification, and governing law may differ significantly. Counsel and tax professionals should compare the available structures for the specific project.

Can a trust hold businesses, real estate, or securities?

Potentially, if permitted by the trust instrument, governing law, contracts, lenders, custodians, and applicable regulations. Title, valuation, transfer restrictions, tax treatment, and liability should be reviewed before contribution.

Are beneficial interests in a trust securities?

They may be. The answer depends on how interests are offered, sold, managed, and represented, among other facts. Any capital raise or investor offering requires review by qualified securities counsel.

Does USABA create investment products or sell securities?

No. USABA is not a broker-dealer, investment adviser, law firm, or CPA firm. We provide business consulting, planning support, and coordination with qualified independent professionals.

Which state should govern the trust?

The choice depends on the trust’s purpose, assets, participants, operations, tax considerations, trustee location, governing law, and administrative requirements. Qualified counsel should make the jurisdictional recommendation.
